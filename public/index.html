Rebate-Ready Garden sells ready-made garden plans. Here’s the gap they fill: the rebate money is real — Prince George’s County pays $10 a square foot for rain gardens, and Montgomery County’s program pays the same. But the rules, plant lists, and paperwork stop most homeowners before they start. Inside every plan

  • Plant-by-Numbers map — a scaled layout showing exactly where each plant goes. Plant straight from the page, no design skills needed.
  • Native plant list — species matched to your program’s approved list, with exact counts for your garden size and bloom times so something’s flowering all season.
  • Compliance packet — the program’s requirements turned into a plain checklist: depths, mulch, setbacks, and the photos and receipts they’ll ask for.
  • Submission sheet — what to send the county and in what order, with your plan details filled in. You still file the official application — this makes it easy.

Before you buy — three quick checks

1. The spot sits at least 10 feet from your foundation.
2. The soil drains: dig a small hole, fill it with water, and it empties within 36 hours.
3. The spot gets sun or part shade.
If any of these fail, the plan won’t work on your lot — save your $19.50.

Which size? Rule of thumb for rain gardens: one downspout, pick the 100 sq ft plan. Two downspouts or a big roof, go 150. Meadows are sized by lawn area, not roof — measure the strip you’re converting.
